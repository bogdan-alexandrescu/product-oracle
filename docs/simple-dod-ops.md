# Simple Definition of Done, item 5: no Simple edit can leave the data wrong

Every write a person makes in Simple, one row per operation, and the API test (Postgres)
that proves each property, or MISSING. Built from `api/oracle/simple/changes.py` and the op
modules it dispatches to (`targets`, `key_dates`, `additions`, `one_pager`, `questions`),
the chat (`chat`, `chat_actions`), `meetings`, `drafts`, `visuals`, the date and goal
suggestions, and the mock's copy in `web/lib/api/mock` (`simple-changes.ts`,
`simple-chat.ts`, `simple-one-pager.ts`, `simple-visuals.ts`, ...).

Columns: (a) recorded as a change with who and when; (b) constraints checked (a broken
dependency or date flagged with its reason and fixes); (c) review policy (owner and the
decider apply; approver and commenter propose; viewer refused; a run refused where people
only may act); (d) undo restores exactly what was there; (e) the working draft holds it
until the review policy publishes it. Some tables add the undo mechanism and the stale
`before` (409) check.

## Bugs these tests found (fixed in this branch)

1. A chat card refused because its item changed since stayed open but kept its old
   `before`, so every later Apply failed the same way: the card was never offered on the new
   state. Now the card's `before` takes the item's current values (`oracle.simple.chat.rebase`,
   from the apply route and Apply all; the mock's `applyOne` does the same).
2. Apply all's stop reason was the change engine's raw answer (a Python dict with ids and
   ISO dates) instead of its words. Now it is the message ("Changed since you saw it: changed
   by …"), as the mock already said.

## Behaviour noted, not changed

- `accept_date_suggestion` marks the suggestion accepted at once, so readers of the
  published roadmap no longer see it offered while the dates wait in the working draft;
  undo reopens it (`test_every_op_undo_restores_exactly[accept_date_suggestion]`).
- `merge_item` reads its `before` instead of taking it, so a stale-before conflict cannot
  arise for it; it re-checks the items under lock.
- The subagent audit found two mock role mismatches outside this lane's ops: the mock's
  visual revise/request use a flat company-role floor where the API also lets the
  roadmap's decider or approving track owner act (`simple-visuals.ts`), and the mock's
  one-pager remind refuses a viewer-ranked track owner the API lets remind
  (`simple-one-pager.ts`). Left for the visuals and one-pager owners.

## Part 1 — the change engine's ops (`POST /v1/simple/changes`, ChangeOp)

Scope: all 36 `ChangeOp` values (`oracle/simple/schemas.py` ~line 429). Columns://
(a) recorded as a change with who (`by`) and when (`at`)
(b) constraints checked (flags for a broken dependency/date with reason+fixes), or n/a with why
(c) review policy: owner/decider apply, approver/commenter propose, viewer refused, run refused
(d) undo restores exactly what was there
(e) working draft holds it until the review policy publishes it, or "n/a: non-versioned target"

Cell format: `tests/file.py::test_name`, `MISSING`, or `n/a: <reason>`. Dispatch confirmed from code:
`changes.py` handles 18 ops directly; `targets.py` (`KIND`/`OPS`, 11 ops, non-versioned);
`key_dates.py` (3 ops, roadmap working-draft overlay); `additions.py` (`OPS = ("add_item",)` only —
`merge_item`/`keep_both` are handled inline in `changes.py` despite living in the S7 feature area);
`one_pager.py` (`edit_line`); `questions.py` (`decide_question`, `name_decider`).

### Cross-cutting gaps observed

- Across nearly every op in every group, the implementation code genuinely records `who`/`when` (an
  audit event, a `by`/`at` field, or both), but almost no test reads back and asserts the **value** of
  that actor/timestamp — tests check status transitions, 403/404 role gates, and business-state side
  effects, not audit-field correctness. Treat "who" as code-correct-but-test-thin everywhere, and "when"
  as essentially untested everywhere outside drafts/suggestions' day-boundary tests.
- `refresh` (date suggestions) and `read_again` (PRD plan) are the two ops where "who" is **not even
  recorded in code** — `suggestions.regenerate(db)` takes no principal, and `plan.py` has no
  `audit.record` call at all.
- Viewer-refused is untested for most meetings sub-ops (join, rules, comments, approve, end, minutes
  edit/redraft/send, chat mode) — enforcement relies entirely on the shared `Commenter` FastAPI
  dependency and is never hit by an actual viewer-token test.
- None of the chat-action/chat-card/meetings/drafts/visuals/date-suggestion/goal-suggestion/remind/
  read-again ops is itself part of the roadmap's working-draft/publish cycle **except**: chat-card
  apply/undo (writes the working draft directly, gated by role), accept/edit of date suggestions
  (writes the working draft), goal-suggestion accept (writes the working draft via add_item's path),
  and the drafts module itself (submit/approve/withdraw/resume *are* the working-draft/publish
  mechanism). Minutes edit/redraft hold content in a pre-send "drafting" state until minutes **send**
  publishes it to the Decisions page/Inbox — a parallel, meeting-scoped draft concept distinct from the
  roadmap working draft.
