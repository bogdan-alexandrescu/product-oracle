The whole PRD

1Summary

We will build Crew notes, a phone app where field crews write, photograph and voice their job notes on site, and the office reads them the same day. It serves G-2 Fewer repeat visits: repeat visits fall from 18% to 12% of jobs by the end of Q2 2027 . The pilot starts on Dec 14 with two crews. We ask the leads to approve the scope of the pilot and the three decisions below.

2Goal trace

  • G-2 Fewer repeat visits (this year): measure, repeat visits as a share of all jobs; target, from 18% to 12% by Jun 30, 2027 . Main contribution: notes and photos reach the office the same day.
  • G-4 Office time back (this year): measure, minutes planners spend each morning chasing notes; target, from 80 to 20 minutes .
  • G-1 Customer trust (next year): measure, jobs closed with the customer's sign-off; target, 95% of jobs. Contribution: and .

3Problem

Crews finish a job and the office does not know what happened until the next morning. Notes are written on paper or in a group chat, and a third of last month's repeat visits were for jobs whose first note was lost . Planners spend over an hour each morning chasing notes instead of planning .

6Non-goals

  • Replacing the job booking system; Crew notes reads jobs from it and never changes them.
  • Invoicing or quoting from the phone.
  • Chat between crews; the group chat stays for talk, Crew notes is for the record.
  • A desktop app for crews.
  • Tracking where crews are during the day; only arrival and departure at a job are kept ().

7Top risks

  • Crews keep using the group chat: voice notes and templates make a note quicker than a chat message. See Risks and open questions.
  • Offline sync loses a note: every edit is kept, never overwritten.
  • Union reps object to recorded time on site: time is shown to the crew first and used for planning only.

9Problem alignment

Who has the problem, why it matters now, and how we will know it is solved.

9.1Who has it

  1. Crew leads (about 140): write the job note today on paper; want to leave the site without a call from the office.
  2. Crew members (about 380): take photos on their own phones and send them to the group chat.
  3. Office planners (12): read every note each morning and book follow-up visits.
  4. Supervisors (9): review jobs with problems and coach crews.
  5. Customers' facilities managers: ask for a record of each visit, today sent by email a week later.

9.2Why now

Repeat visits rose from 15.9% to 18.4% of jobs between Q2 and Q3 . Two large customers renew their contracts in spring and both asked for a visit record within a day. The crews' phones were replaced in September, so every crew now has a phone that can run the app .

10Solution alignment

What we will build, in what order, and the rules it follows.

10.1Approach

Start with the note the crew already writes, and make it quicker than paper. Every other feature hangs off the job note: photos, voice, parts, sign-off, hand-over. The office sees the same note on a map the moment it syncs.

We build for no signal first. A crew in a basement plant room must be able to finish the job and close it, and the note syncs when the van is back on the road .

10.2Prioritized features

  1. Offline job notes that sync when the crew is back in signal
  2. Photo markup with arrows, labels and a measuring line
  3. Shared crew roster with today's site assignments
  4. Voice notes turned into text with the speaker named
  5. Site checklists that carry over from the last visit
  6. Customer sign-off on a phone with a typed or drawn name
  7. Parts used on the job, picked from the van stock list
  8. Hand-over notes between the morning and the evening crew
  9. Safety walk-round with photos required before work starts
  10. Office view of every open job on one map with the crew's last update
  11. Search across notes, photos and voice by place, crew or part
  12. Job summary emailed to the customer when the job closes
  13. Follow-up visits booked from the job, with the notes attached
  14. Time on site recorded from arrival and departure, not typed
  15. Supervisor review queue for jobs flagged by the crew
  16. Templates for the twelve most common job types
  17. Export of a job's notes and photos as one printable report
  18. Read-only share link for the customer's facilities manager
  19. Weekly digest of repeat faults by site and by part
  20. Admin settings for retention, photo quality and who sees what

10.3Key flows

  1. The crew lead opens today's jobs; the first job is at the top.
  2. On arrival the time on site starts () and the safety walk-round opens ().
  3. The crew writes or speaks the note, adds photos and marks them up (, ).
  4. Parts used are picked from the van stock ().
  5. The customer signs on the phone ().
  6. The job closes; the summary goes to the customer () and the office sees it on the map ().
  7. If a follow-up is needed, the crew books it from the job ().

10.4Requirements

  • R61 () A crew can write, edit and close a job with no signal for a full ten-hour shift.
  • R62 () A photo can be marked up with arrows, labels and a measuring line, and the original is kept.
  • R63 () A job cannot close without either a signature or a reason for none.
  • R64 () Work cannot be marked started until the walk-round has its required photos.
  • R65 () Time on site is taken from arrival and departure at the job's address; the crew can correct it with a reason.
  • R66 () An admin sets photo retention per customer, from 12 to 60 months.
  • R67 () The office map shows each open job's last note time, to the minute.
  • R68 () A job's report prints on Letter paper with every photo and its markup.

10.5Rules and edge cases

  • Two crew members edit the same note offline: both edits are kept and shown side by side; nothing is overwritten.
  • A job is cancelled while the crew is on site: the note stays and is marked cancelled.
  • A photo shows a person's face: it is kept but blurred in the customer's report.
  • The phone's clock is wrong: times are taken from the sync server when the phone reconnects.
  • A crew member leaves the company: their notes stay, under their name.

11.1F-1 Offline job notes that sync when the crew is back in signal

Owner: Lena Okafor · Milestone: Pilot with two crews · Goal: G-2 Fewer repeat visits

Offline job notes that sync when the crew is back in signal is for the crew on site and the office that plans their next day. Today the crew writes this on paper or in a group chat, and the office retypes it the next morning .

User stories:

  • As a crew lead, I want offline job notes that sync when the crew is back in signal so that the office knows what happened without calling me.
  • As an office planner, I want the job's notes in one place so that I can book the follow-up visit before the crew leaves the area.
  • As a supervisor, I want to see which jobs had problems so that I can coach the crew that week.

Requirements:

  • R1 () Works with no signal for a full shift and keeps every change made offline.
  • R2 () Shows who wrote each note and when, in the crew's own time zone.
  • R3 () Opens in under two seconds on the oldest phone the crews use.

Depends on . Acceptance is checked on the pilot crews' phones.
